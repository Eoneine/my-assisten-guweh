# Additional FAQ - Difa Leroy Gladion

## Why do you have an educational background in Physics but work in data/AI?

My academic background is in Theoretical Physics at IPB University
(2019-2024). My interest in data first took shape through my own
undergraduate thesis in Econophysics, where I predicted stock prices
using a Support Vector Regressor (SVR) - that's when I realized the
mathematical and statistical logic I had been trained in through
Physics could be applied directly to real-world data problems. After
graduating, I deepened that interest through several intensive
programs, including Bangkit Academy (Machine Learning Path), PPKD
Jakarta Selatan (Data Analyst), and a few others focused on data
engineering, machine learning, and AI automation.

## What are your main strengths?

I feel my passion genuinely lies in data - whether that's data
science, data analysis, or data engineering - and I've seen how that
foundation translates well into AI Engineering. A good chunk of my
project experience already reflects that data background, and I'm now
increasingly focused on AI Engineering, particularly chatbots and
automation. I've worked across a fairly wide range of tools, from
Python to automation platforms and dashboarding tools.

## What areas are you still looking to improve?

Communication. Technical skill alone isn't enough in the working
world - you also need to communicate effectively to get ideas across

genuinely weak in, and I've been actively working to improve it:

that explains IT topics, especially Python programming and other
data-related subjects. Channel: https://www.youtube.com/@DLG-x6c

## Why are you interested in AI Engineering and automation (n8n, LangChain)?

This interest grew directly out of my role as an AI Engineer and
Automation at Hacktiv8 Indonesia, where I built end-to-end automation
workflows using n8n - integrating REST APIs, webhooks, and
multi-channel platforms like WhatsApp and Telegram - and developed LLM
pipelines with LangChain and the Gemini API to turn unstructured data
into analysis-ready formats.

## Which project is the most memorable and why?

The Transjakarta customer feedback project. It was a real industry
project, and a tough one - the data was extremely messy, around
200,000 rows, with only a 3-day deadline. The dataset covered customer
complaints and feedback across multiple channels. I started by
cleaning the data and categorizing it by sub-division, then built a
database on GCP (I had a $300 free credit available and made good use
of it), and connected it to a Looker Studio dashboard. My specific
part of the project was the database work and data cleaning - a
teammate handled the dashboard design and KPI definitions. The final
dashboard tracked total complaint volume, resolution status (open/
closed), SLA compliance, top complaint categories, and department
breakdown. I also presented the results directly to the Board of
Directors.

What sets me apart is my foundational strength in data combined with a
background in the exact sciences through Physics, which shapes how I
approach complex problem-solving. AI is fundamentally dependent on
data, so having a strong data foundation means being able to tell

helps me know how and where a model can be improved to fit a specific
need. I think this kind of data-validation instinct is something
that's often underappreciated among AI candidates, and it's an area
where I feel confident in my foundation.

My goal is to become a genuine expert in AI, or to hold a senior
technical role in data or AI - not just working in the field, but
